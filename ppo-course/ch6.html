Chapter 6 · PPO: The Clipped Surrogate

PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) asks: can we keep TRPO's discipline — don't let the new policy drift far from the data-collecting policy — using nothing but a first-order optimizer and a modified loss? The answer is a one-line objective whose case analysis you must be able to perform in your sleep. This chapter is the destination of the course; everything before it was bought to pay for this page.

6.1 The setup

All notation is inherited. Per timestep of data collected under \(\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}\):

$$ r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(a_t|s_t)}, \qquad r_t(\theta_\text{old}) = 1, $$

and the (unclipped) surrogate from §4.3 is \(\hat L = \frac1N\sum_t r_t(\theta)\hat A_t\). Maximizing this naively with many gradient steps per batch fails in a characteristic way: the optimizer discovers it can grow \(\hat L\) without bound by pushing \(r_t \to \infty\) on positive-advantage samples and \(r_t \to 0\) on negative ones — a massive, destructive policy update that §4.4 told us the surrogate has no authority to recommend. TRPO blocks this with a KL constraint; PPO blocks it by making the objective itself stop rewarding ratio excursions beyond a small interval.

6.2 The clipped objective

PPO-Clip
$$ L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t\Big[ \min\big( r_t(\theta)\,\hat A_t,\;\; \mathrm{clip}\big(r_t(\theta),\, 1-\epsilon,\, 1+\epsilon\big)\,\hat A_t \big) \Big], $$

with \(\epsilon \approx 0.2\). Two modifications to \(r_t\hat A_t\), both essential: the clip freezes the ratio outside \([1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]\), and the min takes the worse of clipped and unclipped terms.

Read it as an incentive contract with the optimizer: "You may improve each sample's term by moving its ratio — but only within ±\(\epsilon\) of where data collection left it. Beyond that, no further credit. Penalties, however, you always feel in full."

Work the other three the same way. Note the structure: the gradient dies exactly when the update has already gone as far, in the beneficial direction, as the trust budget allows; it never dies on a sample where the current parameters make things worse than the budget allows (rows 1 and 3 include the deep-violation cases \(A\gt0, r \ll 1-\epsilon\) and \(A\lt0, r \gg 1+\epsilon\) — there the unclipped term is the min, and the gradient actively pulls the ratio back). Both behaviors are machine-checked in test_ppo_core.py (PASS 4–5).

6.4 Why the min, not just the clip

A common misreading is that PPO's objective is \(\mathrm{clip}(r)\hat A\) alone. Compare the two on a sample with \(A \gt 0\) and \(r = 0.3\) (the optimizer has, while improving other samples, collaterally crushed this good action):

  • clip-only: \(r\) is below \(1-\epsilon\), so the term is the constant \((1-\epsilon)A\) — zero gradient; the damage is locked in;
  • with min: \(\min(0.3A, 0.8A) = 0.3A\) — the unclipped branch, gradient alive, actively repairing the ratio upward.
What the min does

\(\min\) makes \(L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}\) a pessimistic (lower) bound on the true surrogate: \(L^{\mathrm{CLIP}} \le \hat L\), with equality at \(\theta = \theta_\text{old}\) (where \(r\equiv1\): both branches equal \(\hat A_t\), and \(\mathbb{E}\) recovers \(\hat L = 0\); the gradients also coincide there, by the §4.4 argument — PPO's first step on fresh data is an exact policy-gradient step). Clipping is applied one-sidedly: only where it makes the objective smaller. Improvements are capped; deteriorations never are. This is the same lower-bound philosophy as TRPO's §5.1 minorant — optimize a pessimistic model of the true objective — implemented with a min instead of a theorem.

6.5 What clipping buys: epochs

The payoff for all this: PPO runs multiple epochs of minibatch SGD on the same rollout — typically 3–10 passes, shuffled minibatches, plain Adam. REINFORCE got one gradient step per batch (§2.7); PPO gets dozens. As the epochs proceed, ratios drift away from 1; samples whose ratios reach the clip boundary in the profitable direction fall silent one by one, and the update self-limits. The optimizer can run greedily because the objective has the pessimism built in — exactly the role TRPO's constraint-plus-line-search played, at the price of a clamp and a min.

Honesty requires the fine print:

The clip is not a KL guarantee

Clipping removes the incentive for ratios to leave \([1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\); it does not make it impossible. A sample's ratio can blow past the boundary as a side effect of updates driven by other samples sharing the network — once past, with \(A\gt0\), its gradient is zero and nothing pulls it back (only the \(A\lt0\) overshoots get pulled back). There is no theorem bounding \(\bar D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}\|\pi_\theta)\) for PPO; the monotonic-improvement guarantee of §5.1 is genuinely surrendered. In practice the drift is monitored (approximate KL, clip fraction — §7.4), and many implementations early-stop the epoch loop when approx-KL exceeds a target (~0.01–0.05): an empirical trust region rebuilt on top of the clip.

6.6 The other PPO: adaptive KL penalty

The PPO paper's second variant returns to §5.2's penalized form, with the coefficient tuned by feedback instead of theory: maximize \(\mathbb{E}_t[r_t(\theta)\hat A_t] - \beta\,\bar D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\theta_\text{old},\theta)\) for a few epochs; then if the realized KL came out above \(1.5\times\) the target, double \(\beta\); if below \(1/1.5\times\), halve it. It works, but the paper's own benchmarks favor clipping, and clipping won culturally — "PPO" unqualified means PPO-Clip. The variant survives as an idea: a KL-against-the-sampling-policy penalty with a tuned coefficient is, e.g., a standard ingredient in RLHF fine-tuning of language models (where it additionally serves to keep the model close to a reference).

6.7 The complete loss

PPO trains actor and critic together, usually with some shared parameters, by descending one scalar:

The full PPO loss (descent form)
$$ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \underbrace{-L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta)}_{\text{policy}} \;+\; c_1 \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_t\big[(V_\theta(s_t) - \hat R^{\mathrm{targ}}_t)^2\big]}_{\text{value regression}} \;-\; c_2 \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_t\big[\mathcal{H}[\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)]\big]}_{\text{entropy bonus}}, $$

typically \(c_1 = 0.5\), \(c_2 \approx 0.0\)–\(0.01\). Value targets \(\hat R^{\mathrm{targ}}_t = \hat A_t + V_{\text{old}}(s_t)\) (the TD(λ) returns from §3.6). The entropy term pays the policy to stay stochastic, postponing premature commitment to a single action; it is the lineal descendant of "exploration bonus". Signs are the eternal bug farm: maximize \(L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}\), minimize value error, maximize entropy — check each term's sign against that sentence whenever you touch the code.

(a) One mechanism corrects for stale data; the other caps how much correction is trusted. (b) Parameters are shared across samples.

(a) The importance ratio \(r_t(\theta)\) keeps the surrogate a valid (first-order-correct) estimate as \(\theta\) moves off the data-collecting policy — that licenses reuse (§4.3–4.4); the clip+min silences each sample once its ratio has moved \(\epsilon\)-far in the profitable direction, so successive epochs self-limit instead of diverging — that bounds the reuse. (b) No bound on actual KL: a ratio can be pushed across the boundary by gradients from other samples updating the shared parameters, and for \(A\gt0\) overshoots the gradient is zero on the far side, so nothing restores it. Safeguard: track approx-KL each epoch and early-stop the update loop past a target (plus monitor clip fraction).

Write the complete PPO loss in descent form: all three terms with correct signs, typical coefficients, the value targets and where they come from, and the purpose of the entropy term.

Descent form: things to maximize enter with minus signs. Two of the three terms get minus signs.

\(\mathcal{L} = -L^{\mathrm{CLIP}} + c_1\,\mathbb{E}[(V_\theta(s_t)-\hat R^{\text{targ}}_t)^2] - c_2\,\mathbb{E}[\mathcal H[\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)]]\), \(c_1=0.5\), \(c_2 \le 0.01\). Targets: \(\hat R^{\text{targ}}_t = \hat A^{\mathrm{GAE}}_t + V_{\text{old}}(s_t)\), produced as a by-product of the GAE pass (§3.6). Entropy is maximized (hence the minus) to keep the action distribution from collapsing early — sustained exploration while advantages are still noisy.

An interviewer asks: "PPO and TRPO solve the same problem — what exactly did PPO trade away, and what did it gain?" Write your answer in four crisp sentences, then name the one scenario per algorithm where it's clearly the better choice.

Trade: a guarantee for a property of the loss function. Gain: think optimizer machinery and update counts.

Both maximize the importance-sampled surrogate while keeping the new policy near the data-collecting one. TRPO enforces nearness as an explicit average-KL constraint solved with natural-gradient/CG machinery and a verifying line search, earning an approximate monotonic-improvement guarantee at the cost of one expensive second-order update per rollout. PPO bakes pessimism into the objective itself (clip + min), surrendering the guarantee but enabling plain Adam, minibatches, and many epochs per rollout — more, cheaper, gradient steps and a 20-line core. PPO wins almost everywhere modern: huge shared-parameter networks, distributed collection, LLM fine-tuning. TRPO-style updates still appeal when updates must be provably conservative and per-update compute is cheap relative to data (e.g. small policies where each rollout is precious or safety-critical).
