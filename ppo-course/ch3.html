Chapter 3 · Baselines, Advantages & GAE

The policy gradient is unbiased but deafeningly noisy. This chapter is the variance-reduction toolkit: baselines (free lunch, provably), the advantage function (the right learning signal), and Generalized Advantage Estimation — the \(\lambda\)-dial between bias and variance that PPO uses verbatim. The GAE recursion you derive here is the exact loop you'll implement in Chapter 7.

3.1 Baselines: subtract before you weight

Suppose every reward in your environment is positive. Then REINFORCE increases the probability of every sampled action — good ones a lot, bad ones a little — and only the normalization of the softmax sorts them out, slowly and noisily. The fix: judge actions relative to a reference. Subtract from the reward-to-go any function of the state:

$$ \nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\Big[ \sum_t \nabla_\theta \log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)\,\big( \hat R_t - b(s_t) \big) \Big]. $$
Baselines are unbiased

For any \(b\) that depends on the state but not the action, the subtracted term has zero expectation:

$$ \mathbb{E}_{a_t \sim \pi(\cdot|s_t)}\big[ \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)\, b(s_t) \big] = b(s_t)\, \mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim\pi}\big[ \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t) \big] = b(s_t)\cdot 0 = 0, $$

by the EGLP lemma (§2.4), after conditioning on \(s_t\) so that \(b(s_t)\) is a constant that factors out. The gradient estimate changes sample-by-sample, but its expectation doesn't move an inch — only its variance does.

If \(b\) depended on the action, it would not factor out of the inner expectation and the estimator would be biased. That's the entire fine print.

3.2 The best baseline is the value function

Which \(b\) minimizes variance? The exact optimum is a slightly baroque weighted expectation, but it is well-approximated by something meaningful: \(b(s_t) = V^\pi(s_t)\), "how the policy usually does from here". With it, the weight on each log-prob gradient becomes (an estimate of) the advantage:

$$ \hat R_t - V^\pi(s_t) \;\approx\; Q^\pi(s_t,a_t) - V^\pi(s_t) = A^\pi(s_t,a_t), $$

since the sampled reward-to-go \(\hat R_t\) is an unbiased estimate of \(Q^\pi(s_t,a_t)\). The advantage form of the policy gradient,

$$ \nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_t \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)\, A^\pi(s_t,a_t)\Big], $$

is the version every modern method uses. Recall from §1.5 that \(\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[A^\pi(s,a)]=0\): the signal is centered, so its sign is informative on its own.

Of course we don't know \(V^\pi\). We learn it: a second network (the critic) \(V_\phi(s)\), fit by regression onto observed returns. Actor + critic, trained side by side. This introduces the chapter's central tension: the critic makes estimates less noisy but — being imperfect — biased.

3.3 The TD error: a one-step advantage estimate

Definition: TD error
$$ \delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t). $$

"Reality minus expectation": what one step actually returned (immediate reward plus discounted value of where you landed) versus what the value function predicted. If \(V = V^\pi\) exactly, then \(\mathbb{E}[\delta_t \mid s_t, a_t] = r(s_t,a_t) + \gamma\mathbb{E}[V^\pi(s_{t+1})] - V^\pi(s_t) = A^\pi(s_t,a_t)\) — the TD error is an unbiased one-sample estimate of the advantage (compare the identity from §1.5, Bellman form of \(A^\pi\)).

Two extreme advantage estimators are now on the table:

  • \(\hat A_t = \delta_t\): lowest variance (one reward, two value lookups — barely any randomness), but biased whenever \(V \ne V^\pi\), which is always;
  • \(\hat A_t = \hat R_t - V(s_t)\): unbiased in the Monte-Carlo part (all real rewards), but variance grows with the horizon.

Between them sits the \(n\)-step family: \(\hat A_t^{(n)} = r_t + \gamma r_{t+1} + \cdots + \gamma^{n-1} r_{t+n-1} + \gamma^n V(s_{t+n}) - V(s_t)\) — use \(n\) real rewards, then trust the critic for the rest. Larger \(n\): more variance, less bias.

3.4 GAE: all the \(n\)-step estimators at once

Rather than pick one \(n\), Schulman et al. (2015b) average them all with exponentially decaying weights \(\propto \lambda^{n-1}\), \(\lambda \in [0,1]\). The magic is what this collapses to. First, a telescoping identity: the \(n\)-step advantage is a finite sum of TD errors,

$$ \hat A_t^{(n)} = \sum_{l=0}^{n-1} \gamma^l \delta_{t+l}, $$

which you can verify by expanding each \(\delta\) and watching the value terms cancel pairwise (do it for \(n=2\): \(\delta_t + \gamma\delta_{t+1} = r_t + \gamma r_{t+1} + \gamma^2 V(s_{t+2}) - V(s_t)\) ✓). Then the exponentially-weighted average of all \(n\)-step estimators rearranges into a single clean series:

Generalized Advantage Estimation
$$ \hat A_t^{\mathrm{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)} \;=\; (1-\lambda)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \lambda^{n-1} \hat A^{(n)}_t \;=\; \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} (\gamma\lambda)^l\, \delta_{t+l}. $$

Derivation of the second equality: substitute \(\hat A^{(n)}_t = \sum_{l \lt n} \gamma^l\delta_{t+l}\) and swap the sums. The coefficient of \(\gamma^l \delta_{t+l}\) is \((1-\lambda)\sum_{n \ge l+1}\lambda^{n-1} = (1-\lambda)\frac{\lambda^l}{1-\lambda} = \lambda^l\). ∎

And the series telescopes into a backward recursion — the implementation:

$$ \hat A_t = \delta_t + \gamma\lambda\, \hat A_{t+1}, \qquad \hat A_T = \delta_T \;(\text{or bootstrap at the buffer edge}). $$

The two endpoints recover the §3.3 extremes exactly:

  • \(\lambda = 0\): \(\hat A_t = \delta_t\) — pure TD, lowest variance, most bias.
  • \(\lambda = 1\): \(\hat A_t = \sum_l \gamma^l \delta_{t+l} = \hat R_t - V(s_t)\) (telescoping again) — pure Monte Carlo minus baseline, unbiased, highest variance.

Both identities, and the equivalence of the recursion to the definition, are machine-verified in code/test_ppo_core.py (PASS 1–3). Typical settings: \(\gamma = 0.99\), \(\lambda = 0.95\) — mostly trusting real rewards, with a gentle exponential fade into the critic.

3.5 \(\gamma\) vs \(\lambda\): two different knobs

Both look like discount factors; they are not the same kind of object. \(\gamma\) is part of the problem statement — it defines which objective \(J\) you are solving, and changing it changes the optimal policy. \(\lambda\) is part of the estimator — it trades bias against variance in estimating advantages for the \(\gamma\)-discounted objective, and at \(\lambda=1\) introduces no bias at all. In practice \(\gamma\) is set by horizon ("how far ahead should behavior look": 0.99–0.999), \(\lambda\) by noise tolerance (0.9–0.97).

3.6 GAE in a finite buffer (preview of Chapter 7)

Real implementations collect a fixed window of \(T\) steps (e.g. 128) that may contain several episode boundaries and usually ends mid-episode. Two rules make the recursion exact:

  • Episode boundaries cut the recursion. A terminal state has no future: both the bootstrap term inside \(\delta_t\) and the \(\gamma\lambda\hat A_{t+1}\) term are multiplied by \((1 - \text{done}_{t+1})\). Crossing them would blend two unrelated episodes' advantages.
  • The buffer edge bootstraps. The window ending is not the episode ending: at \(t = T-1\) use \(V(s_T)\) of the actual next state as the tail estimate.
# rewards, values, dones: (T,) arrays; next_value, next_done: scalars
adv, last = zeros(T), 0
for t in reversed(range(T)):
    nonterminal = 1 - (dones[t+1] if t < T-1 else next_done)
    v_next      = (values[t+1] if t < T-1 else next_value)
    delta   = rewards[t] + gamma * v_next * nonterminal - values[t]
    last    = delta + gamma * lam * nonterminal * last
    adv[t]  = last
returns = adv + values        # the critic's regression targets
The exact loop from code/ppo.py, verified against the brute-force definition in test_ppo_core.py. Note returns = adv + values: the value targets come out of GAE for free (they are TD(λ) returns).

Which of the two changes the objective being optimized?

Myopia is a property of the objective: rewards \(k\) steps ahead are worth \(\gamma^k\), so the cure is raising \(\gamma\) (e.g. 0.99 → 0.999), extending the effective horizon \(1/(1-\gamma)\) from 100 to 1000 steps. Raising \(\lambda\) only makes the estimate of the current \(\gamma\)-discounted advantage less biased and more noisy; it cannot make the agent value the future more.
