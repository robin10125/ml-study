Proximal Policy Optimization, from first principles

A rigorous, recall-driven course. By the end you will have derived the policy gradient theorem, GAE, the performance difference lemma, TRPO's natural-gradient trust region, and PPO's clipped surrogate — and written a PPO that solves CartPole, from scratch, with every line accounted for.

How this course works

Reading about RL produces a comfortable illusion of understanding that evaporates at the keyboard. This course is built around active recall: every section is followed by recall boxes asking you to reproduce — not recognize — the material. The rules:

  • Attempt before revealing. Say or write your answer first. Each recall has progressive hints (use them — a hinted recall beats a peeked answer), a solution, and a 📖 Review link jumping to the exact section that teaches the concept, for when the attempt fails.
  • Check the box only for honest, unaided successes. Progress is stored locally per chapter and aggregated below.
  • Space it. Revisit failed recalls the next day, then after three days. The sidebar counts are your map of what's still soft.
  • Derive on paper. The proofs here (log-derivative trick, baseline unbiasedness, GAE telescoping, performance difference lemma, natural gradient step) are short — two to six lines each — and every one is asked again later as a recall. Paper, not eyes.

Your progress

The route

Ch 1 — MDPs, Returns & Value Functions. The vocabulary: trajectory probabilities, reward-to-go, \(V\)/\(Q\)/\(A\), Bellman equations, why the data distribution moving with the policy changes everything.

Ch 2 — The Policy Gradient Theorem. The log-derivative trick, the full derivation (watching the environment dynamics drop out), the EGLP lemma, reward-to-go, REINFORCE and its two fatal weaknesses.

Ch 3 — Baselines, Advantages & GAE. Variance reduction with proofs: baselines are free, the value function is the right one, TD errors estimate advantages, and GAE's \(\lambda\)-dial interpolates the whole bias–variance spectrum. The exact buffer loop PPO uses.

Ch 4 — The Step-Size Problem & Surrogates. The conceptual core: why one bad step can end a run, the performance difference lemma, the importance-sampled surrogate, first-order agreement, and KL as the right ruler.

Ch 5 — TRPO. The monotonic improvement bound, the KL-constrained problem, natural gradient via conjugate gradient and Fisher-vector products, the verifying line search — and the bill for all that rigor.

Ch 6 — PPO. The clipped surrogate: the four-case analysis, why the min makes it a pessimistic bound, what clipping does and does not guarantee, the adaptive-KL variant, the full three-term loss, PPO vs TRPO.

Ch 7 — Building PPO from Scratch. The complete algorithm, the implementation details that decide success (truncation bootstrapping, advantage normalization, value clipping, approx-KL monitoring…), a staged build ladder with verification at each rung, machine-checked companion code, and a debugging checklist.

Prerequisites

Comfort with probability (expectations, conditioning), gradients, and basic deep learning (training a network with Adam). No prior RL is assumed — Chapter 1 builds it — but this is a course for people willing to do algebra on paper.
