Chapter 3 · Safe & Online Softmax

One mathematical idea separates Flash Attention from "obviously impossible": the softmax denominator can be computed incrementally, with already-accumulated results corrected retroactively. This chapter derives it, proves it, and drills it by hand. Everything here is about a single row of scores \(x_1, \dots, x_N\).

3.1 Naive softmax explodes

The textbook formula \(\mathrm{softmax}(x)_i = e^{x_i} / \sum_j e^{x_j}\) is unusable in floating point. \(e^x\) overflows fp32 (max \(\approx 3.4\times10^{38}\)) once \(x \gt 88.7\), and overflows fp16 (max 65504) once \(x \gt 11.09\). Attention scores routinely exceed 11. One overflowed score makes the denominator inf and every weight nan — and a single nan anywhere destroys training.

3.2 Safe softmax: subtract the max

For any constant \(m\):

$$ \frac{e^{x_i - m}}{\sum_j e^{x_j - m}} = \frac{e^{x_i}e^{-m}}{e^{-m}\sum_j e^{x_j}} = \frac{e^{x_i}}{\sum_j e^{x_j}}. $$

The shift is exactly free — the factor \(e^{-m}\) cancels. Choosing \(m = \max_i x_i\) makes every exponent \(\le 0\), so every \(e^{x_i-m} \in (0, 1]\): no overflow, and the denominator is at least 1 (the max contributes \(e^0 = 1\)). The standard implementation makes three passes over the row:

m = max(x)                  # pass 1
l = sum(exp(x - m))         # pass 2
p = exp(x - m) / l          # pass 3

Three passes is precisely our problem: a tiled kernel sees each chunk of the row once, and would like never to see it again.

From memory: (a) prove that subtracting any constant \(m\) from all inputs leaves softmax exactly unchanged; (b) why is \(m=\max\) the right choice numerically — name what it guarantees about both the numerator terms and the denominator; (c) above roughly what score does \(e^x\) overflow in fp32 and in fp16?

(a) Factor the numerator and denominator. (b) After the shift, what is the largest exponent?

(a) \(e^{x_i-m} = e^{x_i}e^{-m}\); the common factor \(e^{-m}\) appears in numerator and denominator and cancels — an algebraic identity, not an approximation. (b) With \(m=\max\), all exponents are \(\le 0\) so all \(e^{x_i-m}\in(0,1]\) (no overflow), and the denominator \(\ge 1\) (the max term is exactly 1), so the division can't blow up either. (c) fp32: \(x \gtrsim 88.7\); fp16: \(x \gtrsim 11.09\) (max value 65504).

3.3 Online softmax: merging partial results

The question that unlocks everything: suppose we computed safe-softmax statistics over two separate chunks of the row, \(A\) and \(B\):

$$ m_A = \max_{i\in A} x_i, \quad \ell_A = \sum_{i\in A} e^{x_i - m_A}, \qquad m_B = \max_{i\in B} x_i, \quad \ell_B = \sum_{i\in B} e^{x_i - m_B}. $$

Can we get the statistics of \(A \cup B\) without revisiting any \(x_i\)? Yes. The true max is \(m = \max(m_A, m_B)\). For the denominator, note \(\ell_A\) is a sum of exponentials shifted by the wrong constant \(m_A\) instead of \(m\) — but a shift can be repaired by one multiplication:

$$ e^{m_A - m}\,\ell_A = e^{m_A - m}\sum_{i\in A} e^{x_i - m_A} = \sum_{i\in A} e^{x_i - m}. $$

Hence the merge rule:

Online softmax merge rule
$$ m = \max(m_A, m_B), \qquad \ell = e^{m_A - m}\,\ell_A + e^{m_B - m}\,\ell_B. $$ Both correction factors \(e^{m_A-m}, e^{m_B-m}\) are \(\le 1\) (never overflow), and the result is exactly the safe-softmax statistics of the union. The operation is associative and commutative — chunks can be merged in any order, which is also what makes tree-reductions and "split-K" attention variants possible.

Processing a row tile by tile is just repeated merging: keep running state \((m, \ell)\), initialized to \((-\infty, 0)\) (the statistics of an empty set — note \(e^{-\infty - m} = 0\) gracefully wipes the empty term), and fold in each new tile's local statistics \((\tilde m, \tilde\ell)\):

$$ m^{\text{new}} = \max(m, \tilde m), \qquad \ell^{\text{new}} = e^{m - m^{\text{new}}}\,\ell + e^{\tilde m - m^{\text{new}}}\,\tilde\ell. $$

The most important derivation in the course. From memory, derive the merge rule: given \((m_A, \ell_A)\) and \((m_B, \ell_B)\), produce \((m, \ell)\) for the union, and show the algebra proving \(\ell\) is exact. State why neither correction factor can overflow.

\(\ell_A\) was computed with the wrong shift. Multiply by what, to convert a sum of \(e^{x_i - m_A}\) into a sum of \(e^{x_i - m}\)?
\(e^{m_A - m} \cdot e^{x_i - m_A} = e^{x_i - m}\).

\(m = \max(m_A, m_B)\); \(\ell = e^{m_A-m}\ell_A + e^{m_B-m}\ell_B\). Proof: \(e^{m_A-m}\ell_A = \sum_{i\in A} e^{m_A - m + x_i - m_A} = \sum_{i\in A} e^{x_i-m}\), same for \(B\); adding gives \(\sum_{i\in A\cup B} e^{x_i - m}\), the exact safe denominator of the union. Since \(m \ge m_A\) and \(m \ge m_B\), both exponents are \(\le 0\), so both factors lie in \((0, 1]\).

Prove by induction on the number of tiles that the running recurrence (starting from \(m^{(0)}=-\infty\), \(\ell^{(0)}=0\)) yields, after folding tiles \(1..t\), exactly \(m^{(t)} = \max_{i \le t\text{-th tile end}} x_i\) and \(\ell^{(t)} = \sum e^{x_i - m^{(t)}}\) over all scores seen so far.

The inductive step is the merge rule with \(A\) = "everything so far" and \(B\) = "the new tile". You proved that already.

Base: an empty set has max \(-\infty\) and (vacuously) \(\ell=0\); the first fold gives \(m^{(1)} = \tilde m_1\) and \(\ell^{(1)} = e^{-\infty}\cdot 0 + e^{0}\tilde\ell_1 = \tilde\ell_1\). ✓ Step: assume \((m^{(t-1)}, \ell^{(t-1)})\) are the exact statistics of tiles \(1..t{-}1\); the update is precisely the merge of that set with tile \(t\), which §3.3's merge proof showed yields the exact statistics of the union. By induction the claim holds for all \(t\). After the last tile, \((m,\ell)\) equal the full row's safe-softmax statistics — no approximation at any point.

3.4 The attention accumulator: rescaling history

Attention doesn't want the softmax weights themselves; it wants the weighted sum of value vectors \(o = \sum_i p_i v_i\). Define the unnormalized accumulator over the scores seen so far, shifted by the current running max:

$$ o = \sum_{i \,\text{seen}} e^{x_i - m}\, v_i \;\in \mathbb{R}^{d}. $$

When a new tile arrives and the max rises from \(m\) to \(m^{\text{new}}\), every term of \(o\) carries the now-wrong shift — but they all share it, so one scalar multiply repairs the entire vector, exactly as it repaired \(\ell\):

Per-tile update (this is FlashAttention-2's inner loop)

For each new tile with scores \(\{x_j\}\) and values \(\{v_j\}\):

$$ m^{\text{new}} = \max\!\big(m,\; \tilde m\big), \qquad \alpha = e^{m - m^{\text{new}}} $$ $$ \ell \leftarrow \alpha\,\ell + \sum_{j} e^{x_j - m^{\text{new}}}, \qquad o \leftarrow \alpha\,o + \sum_j e^{x_j - m^{\text{new}}}\, v_j, \qquad m \leftarrow m^{\text{new}} $$

and after the final tile, normalize once:

$$ \text{output} = o / \ell. $$

Correctness: at every moment, \(o = \sum_{\text{seen}} e^{x_i - m} v_i\) and \(\ell = \sum_{\text{seen}} e^{x_i - m}\) with the same \(m\), so at the end \(o/\ell = \sum_i \frac{e^{x_i - m}}{\sum_k e^{x_k-m}} v_i = \sum_i p_i v_i\) exactly.

FA-1 vs FA-2: when to divide
FlashAttention-1 kept the accumulator normalized at all times: after every tile it computed \(o \leftarrow \mathrm{diag}(\ell^{\text{new}})^{-1}(\ell\,\alpha\, o + \dots)\) — divisions and multiplications by \(\ell\) on a \(B_r \times d\) block, every iteration. FlashAttention-2 observed this is wasted non-matmul work (the expensive kind, §1.3): keep \(o\) unnormalized, divide by \(\ell\) once at the very end. Same math, measurably faster. When you read FA-2's paper, this is "reduce non-matmul FLOPs".

From memory, write the complete per-tile update for the state \((m, \ell, o)\), including initialization and the final step, and prove the final output equals exact attention for the row. Then explain why a single scalar \(\alpha\) can fix the entire vector \(o\).

Three updates share the factor \(\alpha = e^{m - m^{\text{new}}}\). Initialization: statistics of the empty set, \(o = \mathbf{0}\).
Every term in \(o\) is \(e^{x_i - m}v_i\) — they all carry the same wrong shift \(m\).

Init \(m=-\infty\), \(\ell=0\), \(o=\mathbf 0\). Per tile: \(m^{\text{new}} = \max(m, \tilde m)\); \(\alpha = e^{m-m^{\text{new}}}\); \(\ell \leftarrow \alpha\ell + \sum_j e^{x_j - m^{\text{new}}}\); \(o \leftarrow \alpha o + \sum_j e^{x_j-m^{\text{new}}} v_j\); \(m \leftarrow m^{\text{new}}\). End: output \(= o/\ell\).

Invariant: \(o\) and \(\ell\) are always the exact shifted sums over seen scores with the shared current shift \(m\). Multiplying \(o\) by \(\alpha\) turns every term \(e^{x_i-m}v_i\) into \(e^{x_i-m^{\text{new}}}v_i\) simultaneously, because the shift error \(e^{m-m^{\text{new}}}\) is common to all terms — that's the linearity of the sum. At the end \(o/\ell = \big(\sum_i e^{x_i-m}v_i\big)/\big(\sum_i e^{x_i-m}\big) = \sum_i p_i v_i\): exact.

FlashAttention-2 changed when the division by \(\ell\) happens, compared to FA-1. What is the change, why is it faster (name the hardware reason from §1.3), and why is it mathematically free?

Tensor cores vs. everything else.

FA-1 renormalized the \(B_r\times d\) accumulator block on every inner-loop iteration; FA-2 keeps the accumulator unnormalized and divides by \(\ell\) exactly once after the last tile. Divisions/pointwise scaling are non-matmul FLOPs executed on regular ALUs, ~16× slower per FLOP than tensor-core matmul work, so doing them \(T_c\) times instead of once costs real time. It's free to defer because every intermediate \(o\) is just the final \(o\) scaled by constants that cancel in the single final division.

3.5 One number to remember it all: the log-sum-exp

After the last tile we hold \(m\) (final max) and \(\ell\) (final denominator). The backward pass (Chapter 6) will need to reconstruct attention weights. Store the single combined statistic per row:

$$ L = m + \log \ell = \log \sum_{j} e^{x_j} \quad\text{(the row's log-sum-exp)}, $$

because any weight can then be rebuilt from a raw score in one expression:

$$ p_j = \frac{e^{x_j - m}}{\ell} = e^{x_j - m - \log\ell} = e^{x_j - L}. $$

So Flash Attention's entire memory of an \(N\times N\) softmax is one float per row — \(N\) floats. Storing \(L\) rather than the pair \((m, \ell)\) halves that and saves a multiply in backward; it is also numerically safe, since \(\log \ell\) is well-conditioned (\(\ell \ge 1\)). This too is an FA-2 refinement over FA-1, which stored both \(m\) and \(\ell\).

Define \(L\), show that \(p_j = e^{x_j - L}\), and explain what this buys the backward pass concretely (what is stored, what is recomputed, and the storage size per attention row).

Substitute \(\ell = e^{L - m}\) into \(e^{x_j-m}/\ell\).

\(L = m + \log\ell = \log\sum_j e^{x_j}\). Then \(e^{x_j - m}/\ell = e^{x_j - m}e^{-\log \ell} = e^{x_j - m - \log\ell} = e^{x_j - L}\). Backward stores only \(L \in \mathbb{R}^N\) (one float per row, plus the saved \(Q,K,V,O\)); each tile of \(P\) is recomputed as \(\exp(S_{\text{tile}} - L)\) after re-doing the cheap \(QK^\top\) tile matmul — replacing \(O(N^2)\) stored activations with \(O(N)\).

3.6 Run it by hand (do not skip)

Watching the recurrence operate on real numbers is what makes it stick. Worked example, scores \(x = [1, 3, 2]\) processed as tile \([1,3]\) then tile \([2]\):

Tile 1: x = [1, 3]          m̃ = 3,  ℓ̃ = e^(1-3) + e^(3-3) = 0.1353 + 1 = 1.1353
  merge: m = max(-∞, 3) = 3
         ℓ = e^(-∞-3)·0 + e^(3-3)·1.1353 = 1.1353

Tile 2: x = [2]             m̃ = 2,  ℓ̃ = e^(2-2) = 1
  merge: m_new = max(3, 2) = 3       (max did not change; α = e^(3-3) = 1)
         ℓ = 1·1.1353 + e^(2-3)·1 = 1.1353 + 0.3679 = 1.5032

Check against full softmax: Σe^xᵢ = e¹+e²+e³ = 30.193;  e^(-3)·30.193 = 1.5032 ✓
softmax = [e^(1-3), e^(3-3), e^(2-3)]/1.5032 = [0.0900, 0.6652, 0.2447] ✓
The denominator comes out exactly right even though tile 1 was processed under the "wrong" assumption that 3 was the global max (here it happened to be right; the exercise below makes it wrong).

Pencil-and-paper exercise. Scores \(x = [2, 0, 4, 1]\) with scalar values \(v = [10, 20, 30, 40]\), processed in tiles \(\{2, 0\}\) then \(\{4, 1\}\) — note the max rises at tile 2, forcing a real rescale of both \(\ell\) and \(o\). Run the full \((m, \ell, o)\) recurrence (4 significant digits) and report the final output \(o/\ell\). Verify against direct softmax-weighted average.

Tile 1: \(m=2\), \(\ell = e^0 + e^{-2}\), \(o = 1\cdot 10 + e^{-2}\cdot 20\). Tile 2's local stats: \(\tilde m = 4\), \(\tilde \ell = 1 + e^{-3}\), \(\tilde o = 30 + e^{-3}\cdot 40\).
Merge with \(\alpha = e^{2-4} = 0.1353\): multiply both old \(\ell\) and old \(o\) by \(\alpha\), then add the new tile's contributions (already shifted by 4).

Tile 1: \(m{=}2\), \(\ell = 1 + 0.1353 = 1.1353\), \(o = 10 + 2.7067 = 12.707\).
Tile 2: \(m^{\text{new}}{=}4\), \(\alpha = e^{-2} = 0.1353\); \(\ell = 0.1353\cdot1.1353 + (1 + e^{-3}) = 0.1537 + 1.0498 = 1.2034\); \(o = 0.1353\cdot12.707 + (30 + e^{-3}\cdot 40) = 1.7197 + 31.991 = 33.711\).
Output \(= 33.711 / 1.2034 = \mathbf{28.01}\).

Check: weights \(\propto [e^{-2}, e^{-4}, 1, e^{-3}] = [0.1353, 0.0183, 1, 0.0498]\), sum 1.2034; weighted average \((1.353 + 0.366 + 30 + 1.991)/1.2034 = 33.711/1.2034 = 28.01\) ✓.

Edge-case interrogation (these become real bugs in Chapter 5): (a) Why is \((m,\ell,o)=(-\infty, 0, \mathbf 0)\) the correct initialization, and what does \(\alpha\) evaluate to on the first real tile? (b) In a masked tile, banned positions get score \(-\infty\); what do they contribute to \(\ell\) and \(o\)? (c) What goes numerically wrong if an entire tile is masked and you compute \(\alpha = e^{m - m^{\text{new}}}\) while both are still \(-\infty\)?

(c) \(-\infty - (-\infty)\) is not \(0\) in IEEE arithmetic.

(a) They are the statistics of "no scores seen": max of empty set is \(-\infty\), sums are 0. On the first tile \(\alpha = e^{-\infty - \tilde m} = 0\), which cleanly annihilates the (empty) history. (b) \(e^{-\infty - m^{\text{new}}} = 0\): masked entries add exactly nothing to \(\ell\) or \(o\) — masking integrates seamlessly. (c) \(m - m^{\text{new}} = -\infty - (-\infty) = \mathrm{nan}\), so \(\alpha = e^{\mathrm{nan}} = \mathrm{nan}\) and the state is poisoned. Kernels must either skip fully-masked tiles (the causal-attention block-skipping of §4.6 does this for free) or guard the case explicitly.
